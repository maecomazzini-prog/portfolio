From a fragmented card market to a digital marketplace.

Kardz is a marketplace designed around discovery, collection and direct negotiation of physical trading cards between people.

01 · THE PROBLEM

A market driven by passion, but fragmented by tools.

Collectors already had demand, inventory and communities. What was missing was a product designed specifically around buying and selling cards.

The experience was distributed across social networks, messaging apps, spreadsheets and generic marketplaces.

DISCOVERYFinding relevant cards depended on feeds and groups.
CONTEXTCard information and price context were inconsistent.
NEGOTIATIONInterest quickly moved to external conversations.
OPERATIONSellers needed a simpler way to manage inventory.

03 · INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

Navigation was designed around intent.

The architecture connects discovery, card context, seller identity and action without forcing a linear funnel.

PRIMARY DISCOVERY FLOW
FEED→CARD DETAIL→INTEREST→CONVERSATION→WHATSAPP
WHYThe shortest path from discovery to negotiation. The card remains the central object throughout the journey.
WISHLIST FLOW
FEED→CARD→WISHLIST
WHYSignal intent without forcing an immediate conversation.
SELLER FLOW
MY CARDS→REGISTER→IMAGES→PUBLISH
WHYReuse shared attributes and vary only what changes.
CARD LIFECYCLE
REGISTER→PUBLISH→DISCOVER→WISHLIST→CONTACT→NEGOTIATE

05 · CORE EXPERIENCE

Discovery becomes a behavior, not a destination.

Feed / Swipe

07 · FUNCTIONALITIES

A connected set of features, organized around the card.

Feed, filters, leagues, teams, decades, card types and display rules.

COLLECTION

Wishlist, saved interests and card-level actions.

SELLER

NEGOTIATION

Conversation context and WhatsApp handoff.

REGISTRATION

Single and batch registration with multiple images.

ADMIN

Price ranges, catalog rules, leagues and categories.

08 · SELLER EXPERIENCE

Reduce operational friction without creating a new mental model.

The seller experience was treated as a product of its own. Publishing cards should be fast enough to support real inventory.

  • Reusable fields for league, team, decade, type and price range.
  • Multiple images without an unnecessary “batch” concept.
  • Inventory actions: edit, deactivate and delete.
  • Seller profile connected to published cards.
